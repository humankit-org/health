How this site works (and why you shouldn't fully trust it)

None of your data is stored

This is a static site. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What the evidence badges mean

  • high Large, consistent meta-analyses. Still mostly correlation, not causation.
  • moderate Meta-analytic but heterogeneous, small trials, or numbers converted/approximated from the published form.
  • low Single cohorts, cross-sectional snapshots, or indirect citations.

How it works

Each input maps to a hazard ratio (HR) from a published study, e.g. meeting exercise guidelines corresponds to HR 0.69, a 31% lower mortality hazard. HRs are combined multiplicatively, assuming independence. Real lifestyle effects overlap, so this overstates combined benefits. We therefore cap the total at a 55% hazard reduction (HR 0.45) and a 4× increase (HR 4.0)

HR → years of life expectancy uses a Gompertz approximation (adult mortality risk doubles roughly every 7 years). Cross-checked against two published results: our exercise HRs reproduce Moore 2012's measured +4.5 y, and the smoking HR reproduces Jha 2013's ">10 years lost".

Correlation does not equal causation!

Real lifestyle effects overlap. exercisers generally sleep better, so both the exercise and sleep studies show positive results, but multiplying them is likely wrong. In a similar vein, 8 hours of sleep might be neutral for you but correlates with being an exerciser, who is already healthy. These numbers are hard to work with, and multiplying them assumes no overlap at all.

This math is NOT a doctor

A lot of mistakes are likely being made in the calculation. There's no science out there saying you can just multiply hazard ratios together or do what this site does. You should take everything here with a grain of salt. It's just a bit of insight into what kind of effects different choices statistically have on your health. There are confounding residuals everywhere.

We do try to mitigate this in a couple of ways. Each estimate carries a confidence interval (CI) from its source study, which we combine in quadrature (log-space, assuming independent errors) and widen them for weaker evidence (×1 high, ×1.5 moderate, ×2.25 low), so the final ranges try to reflect how uncertain we are. The central estimate gets clamped first (0.45–4.0), then the uncertainty is applied around the clamped value. And measured values (VO₂ max, body fat %) replace their self-reported counterparts to try to avoid double-counting.

Cognition & happiness

There is no honest way to put a number on these. Effects are small, mostly correlational, and often domain-specific (e.g. creatine helps short-term memory and reasoning, mainly in vegetarians and older adults). So we show qualitative bands with a deliberately fuzzy marker, and the marker gets fuzzier for every low-evidence input currently contributing. Nothing here should be read as "your brain score".
