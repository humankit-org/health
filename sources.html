What the evidence badges mean

  • high Large, consistent meta-analyses. Still mostly correlation, not causation.
  • moderate Meta-analytic but heterogeneous, small trials, or numbers converted/approximated from the published form.
  • low Single cohorts, cross-sectional snapshots, or indirect citations.

How it works

Each input maps to a hazard ratio (HR) from a published study, e.g. meeting exercise guidelines corresponds to HR 0.69, a 31% lower mortality hazard. Sometimes the numbers differ from the study, as we try to get our reference points (HR = 1) to be the average value for a given input. For example, 20 BMI = 1.0 HR, and 30 BMI = 1.07, but we instead divide and get 20 BMI = 0.93 and 30 BMI = 1.0 as 30 BMI is closer to the average.

Where the studies give a joint estimate — several inputs combined in one published model — we use that directly. Where they don't, we combine HRs multiplicatively, assuming independence, and we are strict about where that assumption is fair: the table below is the boundary. We clamp the total to the range HR 0.45–4.0 so no combination can claim more than the studies support.

HR → years uses a Gompertz approximation (adult mortality risk doubles roughly every 7 years).

Where multiplying risks is fair — and where it isn't

Combining HRs under an independence assumption is a published WHO/GBD-family comparative-risk-assessment method — valid when the factors are weakly correlated and do not share pathways (Ezzati 2003, Lancet 362:271–80; DOI). That qualifier is the whole conflation problem, so we hold every combination we make against it:

Zone Rule Where it applies Status
Fair multiplication multiply marginal HRs, uncertainty only across clusters (movement × diet × mind × substances × sleep: different exposure domains, modestly correlated) keep as-is
Fair multiplication coefficients from the published joint model (no per-slider multiplication) within clusters inside published joint models (PURE diet-score components; PA×strength cells) Phase 1/3: replace
Unfair multiplication within clusters with no joint model — marginal HRs multiplied diet sliders outside the chosen score (coffee, magnesium), movement once Ekelund/Momma cells are in, until then the whole cluster must be replaced by joint estimates
No combination per-lever only; no cluster total, no combined claim psychosocial (purpose, stress, social, sleepReg) unless Phase 1 finds joint evidence; screen→happiness already treated this way adopted

Where no joint evidence exists but two inputs still overlap on a pathway, the weaker one is counted at a discounted rate with a residual set of pair parameters (ρ, a model parameter — its values are next to each pair in the overlap table below). Ranges and bands are assumption-space bounds: how far the model's answer spreads under its independence + quadrature assumptions — the honest width of the estimate, not a prediction about any one person.

In an attempt to be more accurate, some math is applied. Exactly how can be seen in the source code.

  • Studies also give confidence intervals, which we combine in log quadrature.
  • Measured values (VO₂ max, body fat %) replace other inputs to try to avoid double-counting benefits.
  • Psychosocial factors (purpose in life, stress, social connection, sleep regularity) are shown per slider only — the studies give no joint estimate for them together, and the pairwise correlations are far too tangled to multiply, so there is no reliable way to combine them yet. They never multiply into the mortality / cancer / CVD totals; only on the cognition & wellbeing bands their (weak, correlational) points each contribute individually. On the main page they are labelled "per lever — shown individually".

This math is NOT a doctor!

A lot of mistakes are likely being made in the calculation, so don't take the final result as accurate. There's NO scientific basis for the math we do in this site. Take nothing on this site as truth uncritically, and please read the attached sources.

Correlation does not equal causation. Some of these sources just check for a variable and output a number. Exercisers generally sleep better, eat better and move more daily, so how can the study be sure, when measuring exercise, whether it's the exercise, sleep, diet or daily movement that's increasing your life expectancy? As such, multiplying the -20% mortality from both the exercise, sleep, diet and daily movement studies as if they were independent likely conflates and overstates benefit.

Furthermore, some of these studies have a lot more caveats than we can fit into a slider. For example, creatine's cognitive effects are most prevalent in vegetarians and older adults. The real number that that applies to *you* specifically is very hard to find. Even more so when some studies do account for confounding variables, like one of our sun exposure studies accounting for the fact that exercisers spend more time in the sun. If you'd like to help, feel free to reach out on the GitHub

Similarly, cognition and happiness are deliberately very fuzzy, as there's no real measure of "brain score".

Caveats

The reference attempts to be the "average citizen", meaning better than the reference is only better than average, not necessarily "good".

The goal of the website is only to give a collected overview of insights into what kind of lifestyle factors affect your health and by how much, whilst maybe sharing some new information. Is a daily multivitamin 'healthy' enough to offset never exercising? (no).

How inputs are combined

Where published research reports how factors combine, we use a joint model (a cluster total from one study) instead of multiplying each slider's effect separately. Where no joint evidence exists but two inputs clearly overlap, we price the residual overlap with a discount ρ (a model parameter, not a published number).
