How the estimates work (and why you shouldn't fully trust them)

The mortality model

  • Each input maps to a hazard ratio (HR) from a published study — e.g. meeting exercise guidelines: HR 0.69, a 31% lower mortality hazard. Every HR on the calculator page links back to its source in the list below.
  • HRs combine multiplicatively, assuming independence. Real lifestyle effects overlap (exercisers also eat better, sleep better…), so this overstates combined benefits. We therefore cap the total at a 55% hazard reduction (and a 4× increase) — the "capped" note appears when that bites.
  • HR → years of life expectancy uses a Gompertz approximation (adult mortality risk doubles ~every 7 years). Cross-checked against two published results: our exercise HRs reproduce Moore 2012's measured +4.5 y, and the smoking HR reproduces Jha 2013's ">10 years lost".
  • The baseline is US life expectancy at birth (2023, NCHS) by sex. That average already includes people with healthy and unhealthy lifestyles, so personal deltas are approximations, not bookkeeping.
  • "Plausible ranges" multiply the studies' published 95% confidence bounds, again assuming independence. They capture statistical uncertainty of the inputs, not the much larger uncertainty about you.

Cognition & happiness

There is no honest way to put a number on these. Effects are small, mostly correlational, and often domain-specific (e.g. creatine helps short-term memory and reasoning, mainly in vegetarians and older adults). So we show qualitative bands with a deliberately fuzzy marker, weighted by evidence strength — nothing here should be read as "your brain score".

What the evidence badges mean

  • high Large, consistent meta-analyses or pooled cohorts with dose–response. Still almost entirely observational — association, not proven causation.
  • moderate Meta-analytic but heterogeneous, small trials, or numbers converted/approximated from the published form (flagged in the source note).
  • low Single cohorts, cross-sectional snapshots, or indirect citations. Directionally interesting, nothing more.

Known limitations

  • Self-reported inputs in the underlying studies; residual confounding everywhere (wealth, education, genetics).
  • US-centric baseline; no age, medical history, or genetics inputs.
  • Some effects are J/U-shaped; we model the published inflection points only roughly.
  • A few contributions reuse a mortality-focused citation for a mind outcome — those are explicitly marked "indirect citation — replace with a dedicated source" in the data file.

Privacy, by design

This is a static site. Your inputs live only in the calculator tab's memory and vanish when you close it. No cookies, no localStorage, no analytics, no backend — and there never will be a database for health inputs on this site.
